Has UK SORA Made Drone Safety Better— or Just More Expensive?

📰 sUAS News 📅 2026-05-17 🌍 国外

文章摘要

The title "Has UK SORA Made Drone Safety Better— or Just More Expensive?" raises a critical debate about the impact of the UK's adoption of the Specific Operational Risk Assessment (SORA) framework for drone operations. SORA, originally developed by JARUS, is a risk-based methodology designed to enhance safety by requiring operators to systematically assess and mitigate risks before conducting complex drone flights. Proponents argue that SORA has significantly improved safety standards by introducing structured hazard identification, operational limitations, and contingency planning, thereby reducing the likelihood of accidents. However, critics contend that the framework imposes substantial financial burdens on operators, including costs for specialized training, documentation, and compliance audits, which may be prohibitive for small businesses and hobbyists. This has led to concerns that SORA could stifle innovation and limit access to drone technology without necessarily delivering proportional safety benefits. The ongoing discussion centers on whether the regulatory gains in safety outweigh the economic costs, and whether a more balanced approach could achieve similar safety outcomes without excessive expense.
